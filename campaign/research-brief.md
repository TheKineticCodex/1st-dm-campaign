# Deep-research brief — "The Song the Sea Forgot": the best table experience we can build, and how to build it at full power on Claude Max

You are a research team of three, working together and checking each other:
**(A)** a developer-productivity analyst who knows Claude Code, the Claude Max plan, and agentic coding workflows cold;
**(B)** a game/UX designer who has shipped tabletop-companion apps and real-time party games on phones;
**(C)** a skeptical senior engineer who verifies every claim against what actually runs in a phone browser today, and who owns the "don't build this" list.

Propose, verify, rank. Cite sources for every factual claim (docs, changelogs, shipped products, demos, benchmarks). Mark anything you couldn't verify **UNVERIFIED**. Prefer things with live demos or production use over blog opinions. When two options are close, say so and give the deciding question. Write for a smart non-developer who builds with an AI pair — plain language first, technical detail second.

## 1. Context — read all of this before researching

**What this is.** A companion web app for a five-player D&D 2024 campaign, "The Song the Sea Forgot," run by a first-time DM (the "Lantern-Keeper") who is also the app's solo builder, working with Claude Code on a Claude Max subscription. The app already exists and works; this research decides what to build next and how to build it faster and better. We are willing to upgrade or replace the stack if it's clearly better — **but no player data may be lost**.

**The campaign, in one breath** (it should shape every design idea): a fairy carnival — the Getting Fair — stands on the seabed where the tide went out. The fairies "Below" never take; they get you to say yes. Long ago the Sea gave away its song to forget the Moon it loved; every sea-born thing carries a piece. Five players each carry the last piece of something given away (the Sea's song, a grove's song, a wizard's true name, a mother's tune, and one song still whole). Three roads (a bog of jarred voices, a hall of mirrors that keeps your finest hour, an underwater market where a mermaid's tail is for sale), then a finale on the night the Moon is closest, where all five sing and the tide comes home. Themes: promises, songs, pieces coming home, wonder with a cold edge. Tone: fairy tale, not courtroom. The DM's own words: it "brought a tear to my eye."

**The table.** Five players on their own phones (iPhone Safari and Android Chrome — one-handed, in a dim room), the DM on a MacBook running "the Lantern-Keeper's Book" (a control room: run-the-night scene guides, soundboard, whispers, initiative, contracts, the story timeline), and an iPad as the shared stage (ambient screen or a map with tokens the DM moves). Sessions ~3 hours, roughly weekly, ~10–12 sessions total, characters level 1→5.

**What already exists (don't re-invent — improve or extend):**
- Player phone: character creation with a "fortune" quiz; a play sheet (HP, AC, conditions, death saves, concentration co-pilot, attacks and skills that roll with one tap and stream to the DM's feed, ability-use trackers, spell slots by level, rests); a Spells tab (all Druid/Wizard spells 0–3 as cards; prepare/cast); a Bag tab; a Story tab (the Song's pieces lighting up as the DM marks them, kept whispers, journal, a Ledger of finger-signed contracts); a Guide tab (searchable actions/conditions/glossary + guide cards); a dice tray; a level-up ceremony (subclass at 3, feat/ASI at 4).
- Live table events (Supabase Realtime broadcast): initiative "your turn" banner, sealed whispers (private, tap-to-break, some fade after 60 s), contract offers, conditions applied by the DM, a snail-derby race (tap-to-sprint), a live dice feed to the DM.
- DM Book: scene-by-scene run guides with one-tap cues (whispers, sounds), the story timeline with branching roads, NPC cards, a "clue" tracker, session notes, a soundboard (synthesized), the campaign's original melody (a music-box loop with a missing note; the whole song gated to the finale), "level up the party," "a piece comes home."
- Stage (iPad): ambient screen or a map with tokens.

**Verified constraints (do not assume otherwise):**
- **Supabase — Free plan.** Postgres 17, us-west-2. Free-tier limits apply (verify current numbers: DB size, storage, egress, Realtime concurrent connections and messages/month, project auto-pause after ~7 idle days — which has already bitten us once). No user accounts: players join with a campaign code + name; a **device token** identifies their phone; the DM holds a DM code. Everything is behind row-level security with zero anon policies; all reads/writes go through SECURITY DEFINER RPCs that check the token or DM code. The player's device is the authority on its own character; the DM's Book is the authority on the story. Realtime broadcast is used only for ephemeral events; anything durable is a persisted "handout" row so a sleeping phone catches up on next open.
- **Vercel — Hobby (free).** Non-commercial use only (this is a home game — fine). Bandwidth-capped. Auto-deploys from `main` on GitHub.
- **Codebase.** Vite + React 19 + TypeScript + Tailwind v4, ~13,600 lines, four runtime dependencies (react, react-dom, @supabase/supabase-js, signature_pad). **JS bundle ≈ 573 KB** minified (heavy for phones on bad wifi). **No PWA manifest, no service worker** — offline behaviour is a localStorage cache only. **No automated tests** (a manual phone checklist). No router (tab state only). All audio is synthesized live with the Web Audio API — no audio files, nothing licensed.
- **Data that must survive any migration** (schema in `supabase/schema.sql`, `rpcs.sql`, `story_stage.sql`): campaigns · players (device tokens) · characters (build/state/notes as JSONB — this is every player's sheet) · quiz_results · lost_things · npcs · clues · session_notes · handouts (JSONB content, incl. contracts, level-ups, song pieces) · encounters · story_nodes · stage_states · the `maps` storage bucket.
- **Platform realities.** iOS Safari blocks audio autoplay — sound on phones needs a tap (on-theme: "break the seal to hear it"). iOS evicts script-writable storage after ~7 days without a visit — the server must be the source of truth. iOS has a ~50 MB origin storage cap. Table wifi is unreliable; the DM's MacBook may be the only good connection.
- **Design laws already adopted (challenge them only with evidence):** phones at the table must be *fast, glanceable, and diegetic* — seconds not menus, an artifact not a browser; anything that invites heads-down browsing is wrong even if beautiful. The iPad is the shared focal point; phones are for secrets, dice, and ceremonies. Private content is sealed by default (texts on a phone are easy to see by accident). 44 px targets, visible focus, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected by every animation, one-handed use. No paid services. Original content only — no copyrighted rules text (SRD 5.2 / CC-BY is fine), no copyrighted music in the app (Spotify stays outside it), no AI-generated art except the existing opt-in character "Mirror" portraits.
- **The builder.** One person, not a professional developer, building between weekly sessions with Claude Code on a **Claude Max** subscription (research both the 5× and 20× tiers; the builder will tell you which). Also uses Claude in the browser. Wants "the best of the best code we can possibly get" — quality and craft, not just features.

## 2. Research questions

### Part A — Working at full power on Claude Max (for THIS builder, THIS repo)

1. **How the plan actually works.** Current Max tiers and what they include; the rolling session windows and any weekly caps; how usage is counted (models, tokens, tools, screenshots, subagents); what resets when; what happens at the cap. Cite Anthropic's own docs; note dates — this changes.
2. **Getting the most out of each session.** Evidence-backed guidance on: model choice per task (Opus vs Sonnet vs Haiku) and effort levels; when extended thinking earns its cost; prompt caching and how a solo builder benefits without doing anything; plan mode; subagents and workflows (fan-out review, adversarial verification, parallel implementation with worktrees) — when they save time and when they burn budget; keeping context lean (what to put in `CLAUDE.md`, memory, hooks; when to start a fresh session); using MCP connectors (Supabase, Vercel, GitHub) safely; headless/CI runs; the real cost of screenshots and browser automation.
3. **A working rhythm.** Given ~one build day per week between sessions: a concrete weekly playbook (prep → build → verify → ship → session → retro), with prompt templates the builder can copy for the recurring jobs in this repo — "build a feature end-to-end with tests," "verify a rules figure against the SRD," "reseed the Book from the seed files," "review the diff before I ship," "walk the app on a phone viewport and report what's broken."
4. **Quality.** What "best-of-the-best" looks like for a project this size: the minimum viable test strategy (which tests are worth it here — Playwright on the phone flows? unit tests on the rules engine?), type-safety practices, accessibility checks, performance budgets for phones, review rituals with an AI pair, and how to keep an AI from quietly degrading code quality over months. Name specific tools and configs that fit Vite/React/TS with near-zero maintenance.
5. **Guardrails.** How to avoid the classic solo-builder-with-AI failures: silent regressions, secrets in the repo, destructive migrations, over-building, and burning the whole week's budget on one rabbit hole.

### Part B — The best possible experience for the five players

Everything here must obey the design laws above unless you argue explicitly, with evidence, that a law should change.

6. **Beautiful, fun, interactive UI on a phone.** What separates the tabletop-companion apps players *love* from the ones they abandon (survey real products and communities — D&D Beyond, Foundry, Owlbear Rodeo, Role, Alchemy, physical-digital hybrids; and party-game apps like Jackbox for phone-as-controller patterns). Concrete UI patterns for: glanceable vitals, one-tap actions, ceremonies (level-up, signing, sealed whispers) that feel like *moments*, motion that helps rather than distracts, typography and contrast for a dim room, haptics where the platform allows. Where should the app be *more* beautiful, and where should it get out of the way?
7. **In-app games players play with each other while we play — carnival games.** Session 1 was literally at a carnival; a tap-to-sprint snail derby exists. Research real-time multi-phone minigame design: which mechanics work in ~60 seconds on phones with unreliable wifi (tap races, timing games, rhythm, guessing, drawing, auction/bidding, cooperative "hold the line" games), how to render shared state on the iPad, fairness/latency handling, and how to make each game *diegetic* to the Getting Fair (a fortune wheel, ring toss for lanterns, catch-the-pixie, a strong-man bell, a shell game run by a fairy who cheats politely, a "bargain" bidding game). For each: the mechanic, what the phone shows vs the iPad, how it ties into the fiction (prizes the DM pre-loads), and honest effort. Also: table-etiquette risks (games that pull attention from the story) and how to keep them short.
8. **Battles that make players feel they are *there*.** The current battle tools are an initiative banner on phones, DM-moved tokens on the iPad map, conditions pushed to phones, and a dice feed. Research what makes tabletop combat *feel* present rather than administrative: turn awareness (the "tap on the shoulder"), threat and stakes made visible, the shared iPad as a living battlefield (lighting, weather, camera moves, token animation, fog), sound and music per phase, phones showing exactly what THIS player can do right now (their actions, targets in reach, resource state), fast HP/condition entry, death-save drama, and post-battle "what just happened" recaps. Look at what VTTs, board games with apps (Gloomhaven/Frosthaven digital, Descent: Legends of the Dark, Mansions of Madness), and immersive theatre do. Give a design for "battle mode" across the three screens (phone / iPad / DM MacBook) that fits our authority model (DM's Book drives; phones own their character; iPad displays), with a phased build order.
9. **Sound and music.** We synthesize everything; the campaign's melody is central (a music-box loop with a missing note; the whole song is heard once, at the finale). Research: the best free/open approach to ambient beds and a mixer in the browser (Tone.js vs raw Web Audio; algorithmic reverb; crossfades; ducking under whispers), free-licensed sound sources and their license terms (Freesound CC0, Pixabay, BBC Sound Effects archive — confirm the current terms), whether phones can safely play sound (iOS tap-to-unlock patterns), and how a "the melody on every phone at once" finale moment could work technically.
10. **Immersion beyond screens.** The Moon as the campaign's clock (bigger and wronger every session) — how might it live on phones and the iPad? Ambient lighting the DM controls (smart bulbs, cheap and free-to-integrate)? Physical props that pair with the app (printed contracts, wax seals, a lantern box already exists)? Post-session recaps sent to phones? Anything else that has been shown to make a table lean in — with sources.
11. **The stack decision.** Given everything above: stay on Vite/React/TS + Supabase + Vercel and improve (PWA, service worker, tests, bundle splitting, a design system) — or upgrade/replace? Evaluate seriously and fairly: staying; adding a real-time layer better suited to minigames (Supabase Realtime presence/channels vs Liveblocks/PartyKit/Ably free tiers vs a tiny hosted WebSocket); a framework change (Next.js / Remix / SvelteKit / Solid) and whether it buys anything here; hosting alternatives (Cloudflare Pages/Workers, Netlify) and their free tiers; whether Supabase Free is enough for the whole campaign or a paid tier / keep-alive is warranted; a design system (Radix/shadcn/Tailwind alone/Panda). Recommend one path. If it's an upgrade, provide a **zero-data-loss migration plan** that preserves device tokens and every JSONB row, with a rollback.

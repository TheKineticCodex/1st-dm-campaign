# THE WHOLE STORY — for the Lantern-Keeper's eyes only
### Everything, from the bottom of the sea to the last note

## The secret at the bottom of everything

Long ago, the Sea was in grief — over what, even it no longer knows,
because grief was the point. The Sea went to the only power that buys such
things: **the Still Court**, a fey court of acquisition that never steals,
never lies, and always has the paperwork. And the Sea sold them **its own
song** — the music the tides move to — because a sea that cannot sing
cannot remember what it lost.

That is the campaign's title. *The Song the Sea Forgot* isn't a song the
Sea misplaced. It's a song the Sea **sold, on purpose, to forget.**

But the sale was flawed. The song was never the Sea's alone: **every
creature born of the sea carries one note of it.** Peaches carries one.
The seal-pup carries one. Grey-Gill did, before she sold her name. A
contract sold by one party when it had co-owners is **voidable — if every
remaining holder of the song sings their note in one place.** The Court
has known this for centuries. Their entire quiet project since the first
bargain has been **completing the acquisition**: buying, collecting, and
collateralizing the outstanding notes one sea-born creature at a time.

## The villain

The Still Court sits BELOW — a courtroom that is also a vault that is also
a carnival. Its power: it discovered that anyone will sell anything if the
price names something they believe they'll never miss. Centuries of lawful
purchases made it a treasury of the intangible: names, voices, springs,
faces, laughs, convictions, vows. Its agents:

- **The Appraiser** — prices things. Hooded, drinks nothing, writes always.
- **The Buyer** — closes. Polite, punctual, unseen until late. Never lies.
- **The Twins of the Toll** — gather honest answers at every gate. (Those
  answers are DEPOSITIONS. In Act 3 the Court produces them as evidence of
  informed consent.)
- **Salt-Mother Naretha** — runs the Under-Sea consignment market. Loves a
  contested contract more than she loves the Court. Future chaotic ally.
- **Master Vetrine** — curates perfect performances in the Hall of Facets.
  Wants Billy's stage-smile. Is un-writing Billy's mentor page by page —
  buying a dead man posthumously.
- **Old Cassia** — bog-wife hoarding stolen voices in jars; behind on her
  tithe; will deal.
- **The Ferryman of Receipts** — collects the tithes that keep everyone
  BELOW in debt.

The Court's one law is its weakness: **it must be legal.** It can be
out-lawyered. Killing its agents never works (kill Naretha and the tail
defaults to the Court); beating them procedurally always can.

## Why the tide left NOW

The sea has been quietly shrinking for generations — Wick's charts show
eleven feet of horizon gone in a lifetime — because a sea without its whole
song slowly forgets how to come back. Forty years ago the Court surfaced
near Saltmere to collect (Griff, age eight, saw the lights; the tide came
back before dawn and took his chalk drawing off the seaward side of the
cellar wall).

Then, last week, at Talent Night in the Dry Anchor, **Peaches sang.** The
last outstanding note of the Sea's song announced itself, in public, one
street from the water. Within days the carnival stood on the seabed — and
the tide went out and did not come back. The Court has, in effect,
**impounded the tide** — held in escrow pending "completion of the
acquisition" (the market notice says it plainly: "FOUND: one tide. Inquire
below"). The little girl on Net Row is right: it comes home when the
party's over. The question the campaign answers is who ends the party, and
on whose terms.

## The five, and what the Court wants from each

- **Peaches** — holds the last unsold NOTE. Her tail was seized as
  collateral on the first bargain, priced "one song, already paid," and
  sits in the Under-Sea market. Her alligator is the only living WITNESS to
  the original sale — protected from everything except paperwork. She is
  the case; her song is the verdict.
- **Billy** — his mentor is being un-written; the pages circulate as
  carnival currency. Vetrine wants his smile. And through Tarn — the man
  his bottle blinded, whose glass-flecked dark eyes now SEE the Court's
  price tags — Billy's guilt has become the Court's favorite lever. One
  day they will offer to sell Tarn's sight back. To Billy. At a price.
- **Philip** — the Court prices CONVICTIONS, and a man this anchored is
  worth a fortune unmoored. His file is open; his honest answer at the
  gate goes into it. Whoever he was "following" is a thread the Court will
  find before he does — unless he says it out loud to friends first.
- **Freya** — her vow ("I would risk everything so the people I love never
  suffer what I did") is, in Court terms, a STANDING OFFER. The Buyer
  intends to accept it — formally, in writing, at the worst moment. The
  bench song (Demolition Lovers) is reserved for the session where the
  two Freyas stand against that closing, together.
- **Freya Moon** — the Heartbloom Grove's spring was not blighted. It was
  PURCHASED — filed BELOW in the same inventory as the Sea's song. Her
  pendant holds the unsold remainder: one blossom, one bud of spring the
  Court could not close on. Restoring the grove is the same case as
  freeing the Sea: find the receipt, void the chain.

## ACT 1 — The Carnival and the Three Roads (now)

Saltmere, the Low-Tide Carnival, and the choice at the back fence. The
three roads each hold a piece of the case (the Reliquary fragments):

- **GREEN — the Drowned Bog:** Cassia's jars of voices, the Whispering
  Causeway, and the Tithe Barge with its MANIFEST — the shipping record of
  intangibles moving BELOW. Fragment: proof of the tithe pipeline. Also
  recoverable: Grey-Gill's name is in one of those jars' ledgers.
- **SILVER — the Hall of Facets:** Vetrine's mirror-theatre, the
  Understudy, Opening Night. Billy's mentor's LAUGH is here — reassembled,
  it flies home into his spellbook. Fragment: the pending-acquisition
  catalog (Billy's smile, priced — and the un-writing order for his
  mentor, with its authorizing seal).
- **BLUE — the Under-Sea Market:** Naretha's consignment stalls, Market
  Security, and the AUCTION OF THE TAIL. Fragment: the tail's consignment
  papers bearing the **blank proxy-seal** — the campaign's smoking gun:
  the line where someone signed FOR the Sea, with the co-holders' consent
  conspicuously absent. Naretha must be beaten procedurally (gavel,
  papers, or out-bid) — killing her forfeits the tail to the Court.

Level 2 at the gate crossing; Level 3 at each road's midpoint; each road
ends in its setpiece. The party will likely walk more than one road across
Act 1 — the fragments stack into the case file.

## ACT 2 — The Case and the Gut-Punch

The roads braid together: pursue the Buyer, gather the remaining
note-holders (the seal-pup; the carousel organ — which is the Court's
TRANSCRIPTION MACHINE, built around the stolen song; whoever else the
table has grown to love), free Cassia from her tithe, restore Grey-Gill's
name, re-write the mentor, and defend Freya from the formal acceptance of
her standing offer.

Act 2's midpoint is the campaign's gut-punch, delivered by the Buyer —
politely, truthfully, with documentation: **the Sea was not robbed. The
Sea SOLD. It wanted to forget.** The victim consented. The whole case
wobbles: do they have the right to give the Sea back a grief it chose to
sell? (Answer the campaign argues: memory with grief is life; forgetting
is the slow death — the shrinking horizon proves it. But let the table
fight about it. Someone will side with the Sea's choice. Let them.)

Also Act 2: Tarn's offer. The Court, through the Appraiser, offers to
restore his sight — price to be named — with Billy holding the pen.

## ACT 3 — The Still Court

BELOW, at last: a courtroom-carnival where the gallery bids on testimony.
The Court produces its evidence — including the five honest answers from
the Gate, read back verbatim. The party's counter-case, assembled across
two acts: the manifest, the catalog with its seals, the consignment papers
with the blank proxy-seal, the witness (one alligator, sworn in), and the
remaining holders of the song, physically gathered.

The finale is not a battle (though the Court will make it feel like one).
It is a CLOSING ARGUMENT with a soundtrack: every remaining note-holder —
Peaches, the seal-pup, the freed voices, the organ itself turned — sings
their note **in one place**. The contract, missing its signatories, VOIDS.

And then the reserved music motif — the melody you have been cutting off
before it resolves all campaign — **finally plays to the end.** The song
made whole. The Sea remembers. It chooses to keep the memory, grief and
all. The tide comes home through Saltmere harbor at dawn — "when the
party's over" — the chapel bell rings on its own again, the horizon steps
back eleven feet, and the collateral chain collapses: the tail (its
reattachment IS the song made whole), the grove's spring (Heartbloom
reblooms), the mentor's pages (re-written, in his own hand, with one new
page addressed to Billy), Grey-Gill's name, Cassia's freedom, and — if
Billy chooses the price honestly — Tarn's eyes, though Tarn may decline:
he has grown fond of seeing truly.

The Court survives. It always does — it keeps everything that was freely
and fairly given, and it files the loss under "instructive." The Buyer's
last line, to whichever PC out-lawyered him: a card, and a genuine
compliment, and "The Court will be watching your career with interest."

## The theme, in one line

Nothing was stolen; everything was bought — and the campaign's answer to
"what can't be owned?" is: **a song shared among everyone who was born
inside it.**

## SCENE 1 — THE MORNING AFTER (Saltmere, daylight)

**Read aloud:** *"Morning comes grey and wrong. No gull-cries — the gulls
stand in silent rows along the rooftops, all facing the sand. Where the sea
should sound, there is nothing at all. And Saltmere is out in the streets,
every soul of it, doing what small towns do with the impossible: sweeping up
glass, pouring tea, and arguing about it."*

Then, before anything else: **Maddy's breakfast.** She feeds the five for
free — "brawl champions eat on the house, loves" — and quietly returns their
weapons. (Everyone: open the Bag, re-equip. One tap.) Over breakfast, Griff
delivers the morning's weight — see THE TARN THREAD, below. Run it early;
it colors everything after.

### ⚖ THE TARN THREAD — Billy's reckoning (do not skip)

**What happened:** in last session's brawl, a bottle Billy sent flying
shattered across the eyes of **Tarn**, a netmender. The glass took his sight.

**Saltmere's custom** (Griff explains, gently, over breakfast): no jail, no
mob. *"The one who broke it sits with it."* Billy is expected — by the whole
town, silently — to walk to Net Row and sit with the man he blinded. What
happens there is up to Billy. This is social gravity, not a trial.

**The scene at Net Row:** Tarn sits bandaged in his doorway, hands still
mending a net by feel. He is not angry — which is worse. His son **Pip**
(nine, furious, small fists) IS angry. Beats to play:
- Tarn: calm, dry, devastatingly kind. *"Sit down, lad. You've the sound of
  a man rehearsing an apology. Skip to the end of it."*
- Restitution is Billy's to invent, not yours to demand. Coins from the
  Purse, an oath of service, teaching Pip letters, magic lessons — accept
  anything sincere. The TABLE watching Billy improvise sincerity, mask
  cracking in public, is the point. (His deepest fear: who stays when the
  mask drops. Tonight, Saltmere watches the mask drop. Let his friends see.)
- **Cure Wounds does not fix this** (let a Druid try!): the cuts close, but
  a glitter stays in his eyes. The bottle was PRIZE-STALL GLASS — from the
  carnival crates in the tavern's stock. Court glass. Say only: *"the
  glitter doesn't come out."* Do not explain.
- **THE TWIST — press ✉ Billy's button after the visit peaks:** Tarn leans
  close and says, quietly: *"I shouldn't say. With my eyes dark, I see
  better. There's a wheel of light out on the sand. And little paper tags on
  things — on the kettle, on my boy's laugh. There's one on you, lad.
  Yours says PENDING."* — the campaign's truth, spoken aloud for the first
  time, by the man Billy hurt.
- **If Billy dodges the visit entirely:** no punishment, just weather —
  every shopkeeper's eyes slide off him, Maddy calls him "trouble" without
  the wink, and Pip follows the party all day at a distance, glaring. The
  town remembers. So will the Court.

**Where the thread goes later (for your eyes):** the Court can restore
Tarn's sight — for a price — and will offer, through the Appraiser, at the
worst possible moment, with Billy's guilt as the lever. Meanwhile Tarn's
dark-sight makes him Saltmere's one true seer: your clue dispenser for the
whole campaign. Billy's accident made the town an oracle. That's the game.

### 🗺 THE EXPLORATION MENU (let them wander; ~10 min per stop)

Answer "what do we do till dark?" with: *"Saltmere's yours. Where do you
go?"* Point at the town map if you generated it. Every stop has a find:

- **The market / notice board:** a fresh notice nobody remembers posting:
  "FOUND: one tide. Inquire below." (In the same careful hand as the tags.)
- **The lighthouse:** keeper Wick, unslept, shows charts: the horizon is
  eleven feet closer than his grandfather's charts say. *"The sea didn't go
  out. It got SMALLER."*
- **Harbormaster's office:** Griff's forty years of tide logs — one page
  torn out, the week he was eight. He tore it himself, age nine, so he'd
  stop looking at it. He'll say so if asked straight.
- **The chapel:** the wave-bell rang itself at every tide turn for a
  century. Silent since the tide left. If Freya Moon (or anyone) touches
  it: one soft note — the bell is *waiting*, not broken.
- **The chandlery:** owner will sell anything except rope to people who say
  they're "going out there." *"Rope's for coming BACK, and I'd have to look
  your mothers in the eye."* (Free line: they can have rope if they promise
  to come back. Make them say it.)
- **Widow's Walk:** a spyglass on the rail and a cup of tea beside it,
  still warm, owner nowhere. Through the glass: the carnival, and — DEX or
  Perception DC 12 — one figure standing motionless at its gate, facing
  town. It does not move. All day.
- **Net Row:** Tarn's street (see above). A little girl tells anyone who'll
  listen: *"The tide's not lost. It went to the party. It'll come home when
  the party's over."* She's right.
- **The salt sheds → 🐚 THE SCRATCHER (small event, Peaches's moment):**
  scratching inside a locked shed. Within: a stranded moon-grey seal-pup —
  wrong-colored, too long, eyes like tide pools — tangled in netting,
  terrified. Freeing it takes gentleness (Animal Handling DC 10, or Peaches
  auto-succeeds — it goes still the moment it sees her). Freed, it hums
  THREE NOTES of the sea-song — with the hole in it — then flop-runs for
  the sand toward the carnival lights. **Press ✉ Peaches's button here.**
- **The stranded boats → 🦀 SMALL BATTLE A, "The Stranded Things":** by the
  tide pools, three big pale crabs (SRD **Giant Crab**: AC 15, HP 13, +3,
  1d6+1 grapple `// VERIFY`) have cornered a fisher-kid on a hull. They're
  not evil — they're beached and terrified. Fight them, or herd them (any
  clever plan + a check, DC 10-12) into the deep pool where they belong.
  Herding earns the town's awe; the kid names a crab after whoever saved him.
- **The wreck of the Kindly Debt → 🪓 SMALL BATTLE B, "The Wreckers":** two
  inlanders with prybars (SRD **Bandit**: AC 12, HP 11, +3, 1d6+1
  `// VERIFY`) are stripping the stranded boats. Talk works (they scare
  easy); fight is short. Either way they let slip: *"Fella inland pays coin
  for carnival glass. Old coins. Weird coins."* The Appraiser's already
  buying salvage. First hint the Court has agents ashore.

### 🚶 IF THEY SPLIT UP (solo vignettes — narrate to that player alone, or whisper via Vault)

- **Peaches alone:** wet, small, bare footprints cross her path on dry
  cobbles — mid-stride, they stop. The alligator stares at where they end.
- **Billy alone:** a page of blank paper blows against his leg and will not
  blow away. When he picks it up, faint pencil: a seating chart. One chair
  circled.
- **Philip alone:** in a shop window's reflection, his reflection walks a
  half-step behind him. When he stops dead — it takes one more step.
- **Freya alone:** she catches a stranger writing in a small book while
  watching her. The page (if she takes it — he doesn't resist): a list of
  names she'd die for, in an unfamiliar hand, with sums next to each.
  The stranger apologizes politely and is gone when she looks up.
- **Freya Moon alone:** every flower she passes turns its head to face her,
  like sunflowers. One tucked in a window box is made of pale glass. It is
  priced: a tiny tag, blank.

## SCENE 2 — DUSK: THE LANTERNS (press play on the night)

**Read aloud:** *"The sun goes down behind the hills, and the carnival
lights come UP — and with them, drifting down the streets of Saltmere like
they know the way, come five paper lanterns. One finds each of you, wherever
you are. Your name is on it. The ink is wet. It tugs, gently, toward the
sand — like a dog on a lead."*

Go around the table: **"Why do you follow yours?"** (Their lantern hooks:
Peaches's smells of home; Billy's holds an unknown page in his mentor's
hand; Philip's is addressed to the man he fears he's stopped being; Freya's
bears the names of the people she protects; Freya Moon's made her pendant
open one petal.)

Then the walk: harbor stairs, a mile of shining sand, the path of reflected
light, stranded starfish, the seal-pup's flipper-tracks alongside them if
they freed it. Keep it quiet. Let the table hush itself.
**🎭 NOW upload the carnival map to the stage.** The iPad becoming the
carnival as they arrive at the gate is your reveal.

## 🎵 THE THREE GATES — the ending, step by step

1. Read the gates: green lanterns and moss (the Bog) · silver lanterns and
   your own face (the Facets) · blue lanterns and stairs into waiting water
   (the Under-Sea). Hush's slate: **ONE OPENS TONIGHT. THE OTHERS REMEMBER
   BEING CHOSEN LAST.**
2. **Start "the lakes" LOW** the moment the arguing begins. Say nothing
   about it. Let it loop soft under the whole debate.
3. Let them argue for real — each road pulls at someone's Lost Thing. Do
   not rescue them from the choice. Someone gets told "we'll come back for
   yours." That sentence is the campaign.
4. The moment they commit: **TURN IT UP.** Fire **✉ ALL: the Feywild
   notices** (Level 2 blooms on every phone). Read the crossing over the
   swell if you must. Then not one more word.
5. Lanterns snuffed. Lights down. The song plays them out. **The music ends
   the session — not you.**

## 🎭 WHO YOU ARE TONIGHT (roleplay crib sheet — posture over accents)

- **Maddy Brine** (tavern): hands on hips, everyone is "love" or "trouble."
  Feeds people instead of comforting them. Sample: *"Eat. The sea's gone,
  not the porridge."*
- **Old Griff** (harbormaster): slow, certain, forty years of weather in his
  voice. Sample: *"Sat here through nine hurricanes. Never once heard it
  this quiet."* If shown carnival anything: *"The tide got BOUGHT."*
- **Tarn** (netmender, newly blind): calm hands, dry kindness, zero self-
  pity — which makes the guilt worse. Sample: *"Don't waste both our
  mornings being sorry at me. Sit. Hold this net."*
- **Pip** (Tarn's boy, 9): all glare, no words — until someone is genuinely
  kind to his da, then instantly their fiercest ally forever.
- **Wick** (lighthouse): hasn't slept; laughs at wrong moments. *"Eleven
  feet! You don't LOSE eleven feet of horizon, you MISPLACE it—"*
- **Brother Hush & Sister Hum** (the Twins): Hush never speaks — write his
  slate lines on scrap paper and HOLD THEM UP (3–5 words, always). Hum
  answers everything in half-sung fragments that trail off. Sample slate:
  "ONE ANSWER. TRUE ONES ONLY." Sample Hum: *"...and the tollman keeps
  what the tollman's told, la-la…"*
- **Grandmother Grey-Gill:** warm gravel, shawl pinned high (gills), knows
  too much and charges for it in kindnesses. When she tries to say the
  Court's name: touch your throat, mime bubbles, look afraid. Sample: *"You
  walked in on borrowed feet, child. Sit. The cards have been asking."*
- **The carousel operator:** hums the broken song, weeps steadily, notices
  neither. Answers questions mid-hum without stopping.
- **The Appraiser:** ABSENT tonight — that's the scary part. His corner
  table at the Dry Anchor stays empty; at the carnival there's a small
  closed tent with a coat on a hook and no one inside. If asked about him,
  every carnival-folk answers the same four words: *"He's out. Appraising."*

## ❖ YOUR FIVE (what to know, how to make their night)

- **Peaches capiche** — Aasimar Druid. *Truth:* tail sold in the Under-Sea,
  "one song, already paid"; the alligator is the only witness and the
  Court's leverage; she carries the sea's missing note. *Joy:* protecting
  someone. *Make her night:* the seal-pup, Grey-Gill's recognition, the
  carousel — and let her mothering matter. *Watch for:* if she sings, the
  carnival turns to look. All of it.
- **William "Blue-Chew Billy" Blackwood** — Human Wizard. *Truth:* mentor
  being un-written page by page; terrified nobody stays when the mask drops.
  *Tonight IS his arc:* Tarn strips the mask in the morning; the prize-stall
  page raises the stakes at night. *Make his night:* let sincerity WORK —
  Tarn's forgiveness, Pip's loyalty, the party seeing him real and staying.
- **Philip** — Halfling Fighter. *Truth:* trained to kill, chose gentleness;
  the Court has a file on his convictions; his gate answer is already in
  it. He was "following someone" — ASK HIS PLAYER who, sometime today, and
  use the answer forever. *Joy:* protecting. *Make his night:* put someone
  behind him in the crab scrap; the mirror vignette if he's alone.
- **Freya** — Fairy Fighter. *Truth:* her vow ("no one I love suffers what
  I did") reads to the Court as a STANDING OFFER; the Buyer means to accept.
  *Joy:* making the table laugh — she's the dangerous one who wants the
  room warm. *Make her night:* let her menace be USEFUL (wreckers fold at
  one look from her), and land the stranger-with-the-ledger vignette.
- **Freya Moon** — Fairy Druid. *Truth:* carries the Heartbloom Grove's last
  blossom; the "blight" was a purchase, filed BELOW beside the Sea's song.
  *Joy:* casting something spectacular. *Make her night:* the chapel bell,
  the pendant cues, and one big flashy spell moment nobody asked for.
- **Table chemistry:** two Fairies = full names on all Court paperwork
  (running gag, future loophole). The party's healers are the two Druids;
  the Fighters are the wall; Billy is the voice. Let each be the answer at
  least once tonight.

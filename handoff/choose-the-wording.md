## The six read-alouds

### 1 · The Gate of Paper Lanterns

**A** — `src/data/nightPath.ts:182`

> The lanterns start a mile out, strung on nothing you can see. At the gate stand two of them: one holds a slate, one is humming a tune you almost know. The slate says: WELCOME. Then the hand wipes it, and writes again: ADMISSION IS SOLD IN SETS. YOU FIVE ARRIVED ON ONE TIDE.

**B** — `src/data/act1Scenes.ts:311`

> Three nights ago the tide went out of Saltmere and forgot to come back. Now, a mile out on the wet sand, stands a fence of paper lanterns, and a gate, and two figures — one humming half a song, one holding a slate that says WELCOME in wet ink. The lanterns you each carry tug toward it like dogs on a lead.

**What differs:** A does the slate *changing* in front of them, which is the scene's whole trick and sets up the toll. B has the recap ("three nights ago…") and the lanterns-as-dogs image, which A hasn't got. **My read: both.** B's opening two sentences, then A's slate business. But it's yours.

### 4 · The Missing Note

**A** — `nightPath.ts:281`

> The carousel turns on its own. The organ inside plays something old and sweet — and every time it comes round, in the same place, one note is simply missing. There is a hole in the middle of the tune. The man working the lever is crying, and does not seem to know it.

**B** — `act1Scenes.ts:421`

> The carousel turns. The organ plays a sea-song. And every few bars — a hole, the same hole, where a note should be. The horses' glass eyes are all turned toward the water.

**What differs:** A ends on the weeping operator; B ends on the horses' eyes turned toward the water. **Both endings are good and they are different scenes.** A makes it about a person who has lost something and doesn't know; B makes it about the sea watching. A is the version that sets up the operator, who is a door in both files. **My read: A's body, and B's horses as an extra sentence before the operator** — the eyes land, then the crying lands harder. This is the checkpoint you stalled at, so it's worth getting the words exactly how you want them.

**Your call:** ☐ A ☐ B ☐ both — ______________________

### 5 · Whispers in the Dark

**A** — `nightPath.ts:315`

**B** — `act1Scenes.ts:461`

A risks saying out loud the thing the sealed whisper exists to deliver. **My read: B**, and this is the one where I'd most gently push back on A.

### 6 · The Three Gates

**A** — `nightPath.ts:343`

> The back fence has three gates in it that were not there an hour ago. One is hung with green lanterns, one with silver, one with blue. Hush is waiting, slate already written: ONE OPENS TONIGHT. THE OTHERS REMEMBER BEING CHOSEN LAST.

**B** — `act1Scenes.ts:527`

> The back fence isn't a fence anymore. It's three gates. Green lanterns, and the smell of moss. Silver lanterns, and your own face looking back. Blue lanterns, and stairs going down into water that waits.

**What differs:** B gives each gate a sense — moss, your own face, water that waits — which is what they're choosing between, so it's doing the work of the scene. A has the slate. **My read: both, in that order** — B's three gates, then A's slate line as the last beat before the argument starts. The slate is also a stage card (`slate-oneopens`), so it can go on the iPad while you say B.
